At the interface
of materials and charge.

Electrochemical characterization of functional materials for energy-related applications, including the oxygen evolution reaction.

My work combines voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, and Tafel analysis to examine electrode activity, charge transfer, and kinetic behaviour.

Methods reference

Different measurements. Complementary questions.

Methods used to examine electrode behaviour
Method What it examines Interpretation
LSV Current as applied potential is swept through a selected range. Polarization behaviour and the potential needed to reach a stated current density.
EIS Frequency-dependent response to a small alternating perturbation. Solution resistance, interfacial processes, and transport contributions within a suitable model.
Tafel analysis Overpotential against the logarithm of current density in a suitable kinetic region. Kinetic trends; the fitted slope alone does not establish a reaction mechanism.

The reaction

Oxygen evolution

The oxygen evolution reaction is an anodic process in water electrolysis. Studying the additional potential required to drive it helps describe electrode behaviour under defined conditions.

Surface chemistry, electrical connectivity, morphology, and electrode preparation all influence the response.

The comparison

Conditions belong with the result.

  • Electrolyte composition and temperature
  • Reference-electrode scale
  • Material loading and electrode area
  • Scan rate and measurement potential
  • Resistance correction and normalization

From a curve to a defensible interpretation.

My analysis connects current–potential relationships with impedance measurements and material characteristics. A larger current alone does not demonstrate higher intrinsic catalytic activity; the electrode and measurement context matter.
