03 / Electrochemistry

Electrocatalytic Materials

My research involves the electrochemical characterization of functional materials for energy-related applications, including the oxygen evolution reaction. I use voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, and Tafel analysis to examine electrochemical activity, charge-transfer behaviour, and reaction kinetics.

01 / MATERIALS AND INTERFACES

Functional materials for electrocatalysis

Electrochemical behaviour depends on both the material and its interaction with the electrolyte. My work examines these responses within the broader study of functional materials and energy conversion.

Oxygen evolution reaction

The oxygen evolution reaction (OER) is an important anodic reaction in water electrolysis. Studying the potential required to drive a given current helps assess the behaviour of an electrode under defined experimental conditions.

Factors influencing electrode behaviour

  • Surface chemistry: the chemical environment at the electrode–electrolyte interface.
  • Electrical connectivity: pathways for charge transport through the electrode.
  • Morphology: surface features and the accessibility of the material to the electrolyte.
  • Electrode preparation: material loading, substrate, and the consistency of the deposited layer.

Considering these factors supports interpretation of the measured response without attributing every change to catalytic activity alone.

02 / EXPERIMENTAL MEASUREMENTS

Voltammetry and impedance spectroscopy

Complementary electrochemical measurements provide information about current–potential behaviour and the electrical response of the electrode–electrolyte system.

Linear sweep voltammetry

Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) records current while the applied potential is swept through a selected range. The resulting polarization curve supports comparison of electrode responses under matched conditions.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measures the response to a small alternating perturbation over a range of frequencies. It can help distinguish contributions associated with solution resistance, interfacial processes, and transport.

Experimental context

  • Electrolyte composition and measurement temperature.
  • Reference-electrode scale and potential conversion.
  • Electrode area, material loading, and substrate.
  • Scan rate and the potential used for impedance measurements.

These details are essential for meaningful comparisons between measurements.

  • LSV
  • EIS
  • Polarization curves
  • Frequency response
03 / ANALYSIS AND INTERPRETATION

Activity and electrochemical kinetics

I analyse electrochemical measurements using current–potential relationships, impedance interpretation, and Tafel analysis to investigate electrode behaviour and reaction kinetics.

Current density and overpotential

Current can be normalized to electrode area to obtain current density. Overpotential describes the additional potential beyond the equilibrium value required to drive a reaction under the stated conditions.

Tafel analysis

Tafel analysis examines the relationship between overpotential and the logarithm of current density within a suitable kinetic region. The fitted slope provides a basis for discussing kinetic behaviour, but does not establish a reaction mechanism on its own.

Charge-transfer resistance

Suitable equivalent-circuit models can be used to interpret impedance spectra and estimate charge-transfer resistance. The fitted value depends on the model, measurement potential, and physical processes represented.

Interpreting comparisons

Resistance correction, current normalization, electrode preparation, and measurement conditions must be considered when comparing materials. A larger current alone does not demonstrate higher intrinsic catalytic activity.

CENTRAL RESEARCH FOCUS

Connecting material properties with electrode behaviour

By combining voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, and kinetic analysis, I investigate how functional materials behave at electrochemical interfaces. The emphasis is on relating material characteristics to the measured response while accounting for electrode preparation and experimental conditions.
