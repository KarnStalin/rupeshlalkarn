01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

How does the electrode respond across timescales?

Electrochemical behaviour depends on the measurement timescale as well as the material. Complementary techniques help distinguish apparent charge storage, resistive contributions, and delayed access to the response.

My approach compares complete measurement profiles alongside extracted quantities, including changes associated with cycling.

02 / COMPLEMENTARY MEASUREMENTS

Three techniques, different experimental questions.

Technique Measurement Quantities examined
Cyclic voltammetry Current during controlled forward and reverse potential sweeps Loop shape, branch currents, integrated charge, apparent capacitance, and scan-rate dependence
Impedance spectroscopy Small-signal response across frequency Real and imaginary impedance, phase, resistance scales, and distributed relaxation
Galvanostatic charge–discharge Potential evolution under defined current segments Charge–discharge duration, apparent capacitance, charge balance, energy, and voltage changes
These techniques provide operational descriptors under defined conditions. A curve shape, scan-rate exponent, or equivalent-circuit fit alone does not establish a unique mechanism.
03 / COMPARISON STRATEGY

Keep the experimental definitions consistent.

Comparison What must remain identifiable
Across scan rates Potential window, selected cycle, acquisition session, and branch processing
Across applied currents Current magnitude, potential limits, segment definition, and resistive-drop treatment
Across impedance spectra Frequency range, measurement state, perturbation conditions, and consistency checks
Before and after cycling Electrode identity, cycling history, and comparable measurement conditions
Across normalized quantities Whether values are electrode-level, mass-specific, or area-normalized
04 / MEASUREMENT TO INTERPRETATION

A connected experimental workflow.

  1. Document the electrode and measurement state Retain material identity, preparation details, normalization basis, and cycling history.
  2. Acquire complementary responses Measure CV, EIS, and GCD using defined potential, current, and frequency protocols.
  3. Check measurement quality Inspect clipping, drift, resolved segments, repeatability where available, and spectral consistency.
  4. Extract comparable quantities Calculate charge, capacitance, impedance, and rate-related measures with explicit conventions.
  5. Interpret across techniques Compare storage and relaxation information while retaining the different timescales and assumptions.
