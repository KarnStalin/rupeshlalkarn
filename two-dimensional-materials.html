01 / Two-Dimensional Materials

MXenes & Functional Composites

My research focuses on the preparation and characterization of Ti₃AlCN-derived Ti₃CNTₓ MXenes and their composites with transition-metal sulfides. I examine how selective etching, intercalation, and composite formation influence material structure, surface chemistry, and morphology.

01 / MATERIAL SYSTEMS

MXenes and sulfide composites

My work involves the titanium carbonitride MAX phase Ti₃AlCN and its MXene derivative, Ti₃CNTₓ. The MAX phase acts as the layered precursor from which aluminium is selectively removed to obtain the MXene.

From MAX phase to MXene

Ti₃CNTₓ contains titanium, carbon, and nitrogen within its layered framework. The notation Tₓ represents surface terminations, which may include oxygen, hydroxyl, and fluorine-containing groups depending on processing conditions.

Composite material systems

I also investigate MXene-based composites containing transition-metal sulfides such as NiS₂, CuS₂, ZnS, and MoS₂. These systems provide a basis for examining the structural and chemical changes associated with combining MXenes and sulfide phases.

Key research questions

  • How does the material change during conversion from the MAX precursor to MXene?
  • What structural and chemical signatures distinguish the precursor, processed MXene, and composites?
  • How are the MXene and sulfide components distributed within the composite?

02 / SYNTHESIS AND PROCESSING

Selective etching and intercalation

My processing work uses LiF/HCl etching to selectively remove aluminium from Ti₃AlCN. This step converts the layered MAX precursor into a MXene-containing material while also influencing its surface chemistry.

Etching and washing

Following etching, the material is washed to remove residual reaction species and prepare it for further treatment. Comparing the precursor and washed material helps assess the structural changes introduced during this stage.

DMSO intercalation

Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) treatment is used to promote intercalation between MXene layers. I examine the associated changes in layer spacing and stacking using complementary characterization methods.

Following the processing sequence

  • Precursor: establish the structure and morphology of Ti₃AlCN.
  • Etched material: examine changes associated with aluminium removal.
  • Intercalated material: investigate the effects of DMSO treatment.
  • Composites: examine changes introduced by sulfide incorporation.
  • LiF/HCl etching
  • Washing
  • DMSO intercalation
  • Composite formation
03 / MATERIALS CHARACTERIZATION

Structure, chemistry, and morphology

I combine diffraction, spectroscopy, and microscopy to examine the changes produced by etching, intercalation, and composite formation. Each technique provides a different view of the material.

Crystal structure and bonding

  • XRD: examines crystalline phases, diffraction features, and changes associated with layer spacing and stacking.
  • Raman spectroscopy: probes vibrational features and bonding-related changes in the precursor, MXene, and composites.
  • FTIR: provides information on infrared-active bonds and functional groups.

Surface and elemental analysis

  • XPS: examines near-surface elemental composition and chemical states.
  • SEM: reveals particle morphology, surface features, and visible layered structures.
  • EDS: supports elemental identification and assessment of elemental distribution.

Interpreting these measurements together supports a more complete assessment than relying on a single technique.

  • XRD
  • Raman
  • FTIR
  • XPS
  • SEM/EDS

CENTRAL RESEARCH FOCUS

Connecting processing with material changes

By comparing the MAX precursor, etched MXene, DMSO-intercalated material, and sulfide-containing composites, I investigate how each processing stage changes the material. The emphasis is on understanding relationships between crystal structure, layer arrangement, surface chemistry, morphology, and elemental composition.
