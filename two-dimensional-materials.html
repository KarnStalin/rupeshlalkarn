01 / Two-Dimensional Materials

MXenes & Functional Composites

My research examines how processing changes the structure and surface chemistry of MXenes, and how these changes relate to their functional behaviour. I focus on Ti₃AlCN-derived Ti₃CNTₓ and composites formed with transition-metal sulfides.

01 / MATERIAL SYSTEMS

MXenes and sulfide composites

My work involves the Ti₃AlCN MAX phase and its MXene derivative, Ti₃CNTₓ, together with MXene-based composites containing transition-metal sulfides such as NiS₂, CuS₂, ZnS, and MoS₂.

  • Ti₃AlCN
  • Ti₃CNTₓ
  • NiS₂
  • CuS₂
  • ZnS
  • MoS₂
02 / SYNTHESIS AND PROCESSING

Etching and intercalation

Selective LiF/HCl etching is used to remove the aluminium layer from the MAX phase. The resulting MXene is washed and treated with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to promote intercalation and modify the spacing between its layers.

  • LiF/HCl etching
  • Washing
  • DMSO intercalation
03 / MATERIALS CHARACTERIZATION

Structure, chemistry, and morphology

I use X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, and SEM/EDS to examine changes in crystal structure, bonding, surface chemistry, morphology, and elemental composition during processing and composite formation.

  • XRD
  • Raman
  • FTIR
  • XPS
  • SEM/EDS
04 / FUNCTIONAL STUDIES

Photothermal and electrochemical behaviour

I investigate photothermal heating under near-infrared irradiation and electrochemical behaviour using voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, and Tafel analysis. These studies connect material processing and characterization with functional performance.

  • NIR heating
  • Voltammetry
  • Impedance spectroscopy
  • Tafel analysis

CONNECTING PROCESSING WITH PERFORMANCE

Understanding how material changes affect function

The central aim of this work is to understand the relationships between processing, structure, surface chemistry, and functional behaviour. By examining the MAX phase, etched MXene, intercalated material, and sulfide-containing composites, I study how material modification relates to photothermal and electrochemical responses.
