MXenes & Functional Composites

I study Ti₃AlCN-derived Ti₃CNTₓ and transition-metal sulfide composites, focusing on how processing changes their structure, surface chemistry, and morphology.

Material system

Ti₃CNTₓ Layered titanium carbonitride MXene

Tₓ denotes surface terminations.

The material journey

One precursor. Several material states.

01 / Start

Ti₃AlCN MAX phase

The layered precursor provides the starting structure for selective aluminium removal and a reference for later characterization.

02 / Transform

Etched and intercalated MXene

LiF/HCl etching, washing, and DMSO treatment form the processing sequence used to investigate changes in layer arrangement and surface chemistry.

03 / Combine

Sulfide composites

MXene-based systems containing NiS₂, CuS₂, ZnS, and MoS₂ extend the study to interfaces between the layered material and sulfide phases.

Processing questions

What changes when the layers are modified?

Selective etching removes aluminium from the MAX precursor. Subsequent washing prepares the material for further treatment, while DMSO promotes intercalation between layers.

I compare processing stages to examine changes in stacking, morphology, and surface chemical states. Composite formation adds a further question: how are the constituent phases distributed and connected?

Reading the evidence

A material needs more than one view.

XRD
Crystalline phases and diffraction changes associated with layer spacing and stacking.

Raman & FTIR
Vibrational signatures that help examine bonding and functional groups.

XPS
Near-surface elemental composition and chemical states.

SEM / EDS
Morphology, elemental identification, and spatial distribution.
