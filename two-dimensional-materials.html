MXenes & Functional Composites

I study Ti₃AlCN-derived Ti₃CNTₓ MXenes and transition-metal sulfide composites, examining how processing changes their structure, surface chemistry, morphology, and functional response.

My work combines synthesis and intercalation with complementary characterization, photothermal and electrochemical measurements, and physics-guided descriptor analysis.

The material family

A layered framework with chemically active surfaces.

MXenes are a family of two-dimensional transition-metal carbides, nitrides, and carbonitrides. Their metal–carbon or metal–nitrogen framework is combined with surface groups that interact with surrounding molecules, ions, and other material phases.

The family is studied for electrical conductivity, accessible surfaces, and tunable interlayer chemistry. In practice, performance depends on how the sheets are prepared, stacked, dispersed, and combined with other materials.

Mn+1XnTx
M
An early transition metal; titanium in my primary material system.
X
Carbon and/or nitrogen within the layered framework.
Tx
Surface terminations, which can include oxygen-, hydroxyl-, and fluorine-containing groups depending on the preparation route.

Material preparation

Track the material through each processing state.

I compare the precursor and processed MXene states so that structural, chemical, and functional measurements retain a clear preparation history.

  1. Ti₃AlCN MAX precursor

    The layered precursor supplies the starting structure and a reference for examining selective aluminium removal and subsequent material changes.

  2. Acid-etched MXene

    LiF/HCl etching is followed by centrifugation and washing to remove residual acid and soluble by-products. The recovered material is examined for changes in phase signatures, stacking, and surface chemistry.

  3. DMSO-treated MXene

    DMSO treatment promotes intercalation between layers. Washing and recovery prepare the treated material for comparisons of layer arrangement, dispersion, and measured response.

Processing–structure relationships

What changes when the layers are modified?

Interlayer arrangement

How do etching and intercalation affect basal spacing, stacking coherence, and the arrangement of layered assemblies? I use diffraction alongside complementary measurements to examine these questions.

Surface chemistry

How do surface composition and selected chemical environments change with treatment? Spectroscopic analysis helps distinguish surface-related contributions from changes in the underlying material framework.

Dispersion & restacking

How do the processed sheets assemble in suspension? Zeta potential, DLS, morphology, and gas-sorption measurements provide complementary information on dispersion and accessible interfaces.

Structure & functional response

Which measured structural and interfacial variables are useful for interpreting thermal stability, irradiation-induced heating, and electrochemical behaviour? Descriptor-based analysis connects these measurements while retaining their assumptions.

MXene–sulfide systems

A layered host with additional interfaces.

My work also includes MXene-based composite systems containing transition-metal sulfides. These systems extend the research from modifying a layered material to examining how secondary phases are distributed and connected with it.

NiS₂ CuS₂ ZnS MoS₂

The questions include phase identification, morphology, elemental distribution, and surface-chemical changes associated with composite preparation. The aim is to understand the interfaces rather than assume that combining materials automatically improves performance.

Composite interpretation draws on XRD, Raman, FTIR, XPS, and SEM/EDS. Structural and spatial evidence are considered together when examining the relationship between constituent phases.

Complementary measurements

Build the material picture from several measurements.

Each technique probes a different part of the system. I combine their information to examine structure, chemistry, accessible interfaces, dispersion, and stability.
