Thermal and Electrochemical Analysis

Thermal measurements

Event selection and transient segmentation define which part of the measurement is being quantified.

  • TGA / DTG: event boundaries, conversion, derivative peaks, and multi-rate comparison.
  • Photothermal response: ambient correction, heating–cooling windows, amplitudes, time constants, and transient area.
  • Reference calculations: keep directly measured quantities separate from calibrated or extrapolated estimates.

Electrochemical measurements

Potential, time, and frequency representations provide complementary views of the electrode response.

  • EIS: impedance components, phase, frequency-dependent capacitance, and consistency checks.

Fit Quality and Physical Consistency

A fitted curve is one part of the analysis. Residual behaviour, parameter identifiability, uncertainty, and sensitivity are examined in the relevant modelling tasks.

Residual structure

Look for systematic deviations and temporal dependence that summary fit measures can conceal.

Parameter identifiability

Examine whether available measurements can distinguish the fitted parameters.

Sensitivity

Assess the effects of fitting windows, processing choices, and descriptor definitions.

Uncertainty

Use parameter intervals and resampling in the analyses where they are applicable.

Model comparison

Consider complexity, information criteria, stability, and appropriate withheld observations.

Physical consistency

Check units, limiting cases, sign conventions, and the plausibility of calculated quantities.

Analysis choices follow the measurement and research question. Fitted parameters are interpreted with their units, assumptions, sensitivity, and physical consistency.

MATLAB Analysis Workflow

  1. Organize Preserve raw files, sample identity, units, experimental settings, and acquisition metadata.
  2. Process Apply measurement-specific correction, interpolation, segmentation, or filtering, documenting the choices.
  3. Quantify Extract features, calculate integrals, apply physical definitions, and assemble comparison tables.
  4. Assess Check residuals, uncertainty, sensitivity, parameter stability, and physical consistency.
  5. Visualize Use clear axes, consistent units, meaningful normalization, and comparable scales.
  6. Interpret Connect the observations to the research question while retaining assumptions and experimental limits.

Thermal-balance accounting

Fuel-energy input, useful output, measured heat-loss terms, and the balance remainder support energy-distribution analysis.

BSFC = ṁf / Pb
ηb = Pb / (ṁf × LHV)

Representative definitions: ṁf is fuel mass-flow rate, Pb is brake power, and LHV is lower heating value. Unit conversions and measurement conventions must be consistent.
