Funded experimental research · Jatropha biodiesel

Testing of Performance Characteristics and Emission Improvement of Jatropha Oil in Internal Combustion Engines

Abstract

An experimental investigation was carried out to study the performance of Jatropha blends the test bench. The fuel thermo-physical properties of Jatropha blend 20% (JB20) was tested. The test result showed that the properties of JB20 are under the Biodiesel Standard India. Cetane number of JB20 was found higher than that of standard Cetane number of diesel and Cetane number of Bharat Biodiesel standard which leads to improving ignition quality by pre-ignition timing. Higher water content in Jatropha blends facilitate in combustion process. The performance of various Jatropha blend with pure diesel up to 20% were tested. There is a small margin of difference between the Brake power of various Jatropha blends and Diesel. JB20 has better performance parameters such indicated power, brake power, indicated thermal efficiency, brake thermal efficiency, specific fuel consumption, and fuel consumption than other Jatropha blends. So JB20 can be used as an alternative fuel in internal combustion engines. All the blends of Jatropha oil passed the opacity test.

Funded experimental research · Waste-plastic-derived fuel

Testing of Performance Characteristics and Emission Improvement of Plastic Pyrolysis Fuel in Internal Combustion Engines

Abstract

An experimental investigation was carried out to study the performance of Plasto diesel blends in test bench. The fuel thermo-physical properties of Plasto diesel blend 10% (PDB10) and Plasto diesel blend 20% (PDB20) were tested. Test result showed that the properties of Plasto diesel are under the Bharat Standard 4 and Bharat standard 6. Cetane number of PDB10 and PDB20 was found higher than that of standard which leads to improve ignition quality by pre-ignition timing. The performance and Combustion Characteristics of various Plasto diesel blend with pure diesel up to 20% were tested. For the same brake power, the indicated power of 20PDB is lower than conventional diesel. There is a small margin of difference in IP of other blends of PDB and Diesel. There is a small margin of difference in Specific fuel consumption of 20PDB and Diesel. Mechanical efficiency of 20PDB is greater than other blends of Plasto diesel and conventional diesel, volumetric efficiency of 20PDB is lower than other blends of Plasto Diesel and Diesel. 20PDB has lower ignition delay and the cylinder pressure of Plasto diesel blends is lower than diesel, all the blends of Plastodiesel blend passed the opacity test. From the fuel property and engine performance, combustion and emission characteristics, it is concluded that any blends of plastic oil up to 20PDB can be used as an alternative source of conventional diesel.

Institutionally supported research · Hybrid biodiesel & modelling

Optimizing Sustainable Hybrid Biodiesel: A Synergistic Blend of Soybean and Sesame Oils for Enhanced Engine Performance, Combustion and Emission

Abstract

This study aims to test and optimize the performance of hybrid biodiesel composed of soybean and sesame as a potential alternative for diesel fuel. This research involves characterization of sesame and soybean biodiesel, testing and analysis of performance parameters, combustion and emission characteristics of sesame biodiesel and soybean biodiesel blends. The research also focuses on using Matlab to construct a model and simulate it for various blends of various biodiesel. Simulation of the model constructed is then validated by comparing with result obtained from experiment performed on test engine. The test engine used was a single cylinder four stroke water-cooled diesel engine fitted with eddy current dynamometer operating at constant speed of 1500 RPM. For the characterization of sesame and soybean biodiesel, FTIR and GCMS was done. Major fatty acid methyl ester component of sesame and soybean biodiesel was found to be methyl oleate and methyl linoleate. For sesame biodiesel, 10SBME was found to be the optimized blend. This is because it had better performance, combustion and emission than other blends in overall. For soybean biodiesel, 20SME and 30SME were found to be the optimized blend because it had better performance, combustion and emission than other blends in overall. On the basis of performance characteristics, 30SME is better while on the basis of emission, 20SME had better result. For hybrid biodiesel, it was found that 20-20SeSoD had maximum decrease in specific fuel consumption by 2.62%, EGT by 9.92% and highest brake thermal efficiency by 3.72%. so, it can be considered as optimal blend but cost of 20-20 SeSoD is higher than that of diesel making it economically expensive fuel. So, we do not consider it optimal blend. The optimal blend for hybrid biodiesel is considered 10-20SeSoD as it is cheaper and also has better overall better performance. Combustion and emission than diesel. Also, it had maximum decrease in Indicated power by 2.46%, slight increase in SFC by 0.75%, increase in brake thermal efficiency and mechanical efficiency by 4.13% in average compared to diesel. 10-20SeSoD also has least CPM with decrease of 5.69%, but has decrease in CHR by 10.50% while it has least NO and NOx emission. NO, Nox and HC emission for 10-20SeSoD decreased by 20.37%, 19.29% and 65.41% in average compared to diesel. So, in terms of emission, it has drastic benefit.

Student research team: Gagin Bajagain, Lasata Maharjan, Samjhana Jyakhwo, and Sushila Subedi.
