Research / Photothermal & Electrochemical Studies

Photothermal Studies

I investigate how material processing and suspension concentration influence heating and cooling under near-infrared irradiation.

The experimental framework connects controlled temperature measurements with characterization of structure, surface chemistry, and dispersion.

Research focus Material series Experimental approach Measurements

Research question

How does processing change the thermal response?

Chemical etching and intercalation alter material structure and interfaces. Photothermal measurements examine the associated temperature response under defined irradiation conditions.

The study considers both the rise during irradiation and the subsequent cooling, rather than relying only on an endpoint temperature.

Material series

Follow the processing sequence.

MAX precursor

Ti₃AlCN provides the precursor reference for comparing processing-dependent changes.

Acid-etched MXene

Ti₃CNTₓ represents the etched material state, with structure and surface chemistry examined through complementary characterization.

DMSO-treated MXene

The treated state extends the comparison to processing-related changes in stacking, interfaces, and dispersion.

Experimental approach

Measure the complete irradiation cycle.

  1. Prepare the suspensions Retain material identity, concentration, suspension volume, and preparation history.
  2. Establish the baseline Record the initial temperature and maintain consistent vessel and irradiation geometry.
  3. Apply near-infrared irradiation Track the temperature continuously during the defined laser-on interval.
  4. Record cooling Continue measuring after switch-off to characterize the return toward ambient.
  5. Measure the water reference Use comparable conditions to distinguish the suspension response from the reference background.

Quantities of interest

Read the transient at several levels.

Observation What it describes Interpretation
Temperature rise Change relative to the ambient baseline Finite-time heating response
Heating profile Evolution during irradiation Rate and shape of the temperature increase
Cooling profile Evolution after switch-off Effective loss-controlled response
Concentration comparison Response across suspension loadings Condition-dependent behaviour
Processing comparison Response across the material series Context for comparison with characterization and dispersion measurements
A larger measured temperature rise alone does not establish a larger intrinsic conversion efficiency. Geometry, thermal capacity, heat loss, concentration, and absorbed optical power affect interpretation.
