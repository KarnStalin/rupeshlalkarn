02 / Photothermal conversion

Light in.
Heat out.

Laser-induced heating in MAX phases and MXenes.

I investigate temperature evolution and net photothermal heat generation under near-infrared irradiation, comparing materials at different processing stages.

Following an experiment

The sequence connects material selection, irradiation, and quantitative interpretation.

MAX phases Etched MXenes Intercalated MXenes
01

Begin with the material.

The MAX precursor, acid-etched MXene, and intercalated MXene provide a basis for investigating how preparation relates to thermal response. Structural and surface changes give context to the comparison.

02

Apply near-infrared irradiation.

Absorbed optical energy can be converted into heat. Laser power, illuminated area, exposure time, sample quantity, and geometry influence the measured response.

03

Follow temperature through time.

Temperature–time curves describe the initial response, subsequent heating, and any approach towards a steady temperature. Consistent conditions and reference measurements support comparisons between samples.

04

Interpret the energy balance.

I use MATLAB to process measurements and investigate temperature rise, heating rate, and net heat generation. Interpretation must consider both the energy entering the system and heat exchange with its surroundings.

An important distinction

Temperature rise is a response, not an efficiency measurement.

A higher temperature alone does not establish higher photothermal conversion efficiency. Material quantity, experimental background, and heat losses must also be considered.
