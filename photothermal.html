02 / Photothermal Conversion

Laser-Induced Heating

My research investigates photothermal heating in MAX phases, acid-etched MXenes, and intercalated MXenes under near-infrared irradiation. I examine temperature evolution and net photothermal heat generation to understand how material processing relates to the observed thermal response.

01 / MATERIAL SYSTEMS

Comparing MAX phases and MXenes

My work compares materials at different stages of processing, including the MAX precursor, acid-etched MXene, and intercalated MXene. This comparison connects material preparation with laser-induced temperature changes.

Processing-dependent behaviour

Selective etching and intercalation can change layer arrangement, surface chemistry, and morphology. These changes provide a basis for investigating differences in photothermal behaviour.

Materials considered

  • MAX phase: the layered precursor used as a reference for comparison.
  • Acid-etched MXene: the material obtained following selective removal of the aluminium layer.
  • Intercalated MXene: material treated to modify the arrangement and spacing of its layers.

Central question

How does each processing stage affect the temperature response observed under comparable irradiation conditions?

  • Etched MXenes
  • Intercalated MXenes
02 / LASER HEATING EXPERIMENTS

Tracking the thermal response

Near-infrared irradiation supplies optical energy to the sample. Absorbed energy can be converted into heat, producing a measurable temperature rise that changes with exposure time.

Temperature evolution

Temperature–time measurements describe how the sample responds during irradiation. The curve can reveal the initial heating behaviour, the subsequent rate of temperature increase, and any approach towards a steady temperature.

Conditions that influence heating

  • Irradiation: laser power, illuminated area, and exposure time.
  • Sample: material quantity, preparation, and geometry.
  • Surroundings: ambient temperature and heat exchange with the environment.

Consistent conditions and appropriate reference measurements are important for interpreting differences between samples.

03 / QUANTITATIVE ANALYSIS

Temperature rise and heat generation

I use quantitative analysis to interpret temperature evolution and evaluate net photothermal heat generation. MATLAB supports the processing, comparison, and visualization of experimental measurements.

Reading the heating curves

  • Temperature rise: the change relative to the initial sample temperature.
  • Heating rate: how quickly temperature changes during irradiation.
  • Thermal response: the overall shape and time dependence of the temperature curve.

Interpreting heat generation

Temperature rise reflects both heat generation and heat loss. Evaluating net photothermal heating therefore requires consideration of the sample, experimental background, and heat exchange with the surroundings.

A higher measured temperature alone does not establish a higher conversion efficiency; the experimental conditions and energy balance also matter.
