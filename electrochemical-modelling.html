Electrochemical Modelling

I model electrochemical charge storage through complementary analyses of cyclic voltammetry, impedance spectroscopy, and galvanostatic charge–discharge.

The framework separates directly extracted descriptors, empirical rate relationships, and physical circuit models. MATLAB simulations connect resistance, storage, leakage, and relaxation times with complete measured curves.

Potential-controlled response

Cyclic voltammetry

Descriptor / Loop capacitance

Integrate the complete response

Anodic and cathodic branches are interpolated onto a common potential grid before integration. The potential window and scan rate remain explicit.

This summarizes stored-charge response across the selected potential interval.
C(ν) = [1/(2νΔV)] ∫VlowVhigh [ia(V) − ic(V)] dV
ΔV = Vhigh − Vlow

ν is the scan rate. ia and ic are corresponding branch currents. Specific or areal capacitance requires the corresponding mass or area normalization.

Processing / Branch separation

Separate common background current

The branch average describes common-direction background, while the half-difference isolates the symmetric response used for rate analysis.

A shared background can bias power-law fits performed on one branch alone.
ibg(V) = [ia(V) + ic(V)]/2
is(V) = [ia(V) − ic(V)]/2

Cancellation applies to a contribution shared by both branches; it is not a universal separation of all electrochemical processes.

Models / Scan-rate dependence

Power-law and two-term analysis

The power law describes current scaling. The Dunn-type decomposition represents terms proportional to scan rate and its square root.

These provide operational rate descriptors for comparable potentials and measurement sessions.
is(V,ν) = a(V)νb(V)
is(V,ν) = k1(V)ν + k2(V)ν1/2

b describes rate scaling. k1 and k2 are operational decomposition coefficients. Conventional surface-like or diffusion-like interpretations do not prove a mechanism.

Trasatti charge-accessibility analysis

Slow- and fast-rate extrapolations provide operational outer and total charge estimates within appropriate fitting ranges.

q(ν) = qout + c1ν−1/2
1/q(ν) = 1/qtot + c2ν1/2

The ratio qout/qtot is an accessibility descriptor. The validity of the extrapolation depends on the selected rate domain.

Frequency-dependent response

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy

Model / Equivalent circuits

Describe resistance and relaxation

Candidate networks combine series resistance, storage branches, and non-ideal capacitive elements. Circuit labels identify mathematical branches rather than predetermined mechanisms.

These connect frequency dependence with interpretable resistance and timescale parameters.
ZCPE(ω) = 1/[Q(jω)n]
τRC = RC
τR‖CPE = (RQ)1/n

Q and n define the constant-phase element. Q is not directly a capacitance when n differs from one.

Descriptor / Complex capacitance

Re-express the measured impedance

Complex capacitance supplies a complementary representation of storage and loss across frequency. Its interpretation remains tied to the impedance definition and any resistance correction.

C*(ω) = 1/[jωZ(ω)]
C*(ω) = C′(ω) − jC″(ω)

Additional descriptors include the high-frequency real-axis intercept, low-frequency impedance, phase, and characteristic relaxation measures.

Linear Kramers–Kronig checks and residual inspection support assessment of spectral consistency. A visually plausible circuit fit alone does not establish parameter identifiability.

Current-controlled response

Galvanostatic charge–discharge

Descriptors / Time, charge, and energy

Quantify resolved charge–discharge segments

Segment duration, potential window, and applied current define apparent capacitance. Charge and energy integration provide complementary efficiency measures.

These distinguish charge recovery from voltage-dependent energy recovery.
Cchg = |Ichg|Δtchg/ΔV
Cdis = |Idis|Δtdis/ΔV
ηQ = |qdis|/|qchg|
E = ∫VI dt

Potential windows, resistive jumps, current signs, and normalization conventions are defined explicitly.

Physical curve reconstruction

From circuit topology to simulated response

Within each multi-rate CV or GCD fit, one parameter set describes the complete family of curves. Rate dependence therefore emerges from circuit time constants rather than independent parameters assigned to every scan rate or current.

Element Representation Purpose
Rs Series resistance Ohmic contribution; constrained using EIS from the corresponding electrode.
Ri ‖ Ci Relaxation branch Access-limited response with τi = RiCi.
C3 Fully accessed storage capacitance Low-rate storage contribution.
Rp Leakage path across the storage network Tests whether a conductive parasitic path is needed by the observations.
CPE Distributed capacitive response Describes non-ideal relaxation over a range of timescales.

Numerical model / State space

Use capacitor voltages as states

Finite RC networks are expressed as linear dynamical systems. The excitation changes between CV and GCD while the circuit equations retain their physical structure.

This supports direct simulation of complete waveforms rather than only summary descriptors.
dx/dt = Ax + bI
V = cᵀx + DI

x contains internal capacitor voltages. Matrices and vectors follow from the candidate topology.

CV simulation: piecewise linear potential ramps

For nonzero feedthrough D, potential control eliminates current from the state equations. Each linear sweep is propagated using the analytical linear-system solution.

V(t) = V0 + st,   s = ±ν
I = (V − cᵀx)/D

Initial state, cycle count, and sampling follow the experimental protocol. Analytical propagation avoids an arbitrary time-step approximation for the finite linear network.

GCD simulation: constant-current segments

Constant applied current drives the state equations. Charge and discharge segments are propagated to the defined potential boundaries, with switching and initial conditions retained explicitly.

CPE realization: distributed timescales

A CPE has no exact finite-dimensional state-space realization. I use a discretized relaxation-time distribution to construct a finite approximation over the experimental window.

The numerical realization is checked against the analytical impedance before being used for time-domain simulation.

Model choice and interpretation

Separate empirical rate laws from circuit models.

Empirical functions summarize capacitance versus scan rate or current. Circuit models instead aim to explain the complete curve family through shared physical parameters.

Representative candidate rate laws

C(x) = C0
C(x) = Ax−β
C(x) = C∞ + Ax−β

x denotes the relevant measurement rate. Here β is a fitted rate exponent, unrelated to TGA heating-rate notation.

Model assessment considers parameter identifiability, bounds, residual structure, information criteria, bootstrap uncertainty, and withheld scan rates, currents, or frequencies. Extra circuit branches require support beyond improved visual agreement.

Cross-technique comparison examines compatible resistance and relaxation scales. It does not assume that every technique must return identical apparent capacitance or share one independently untested fit.

MATLAB implementation

The modelling sequence.

  1. Import and check the measurements Preserve electrode identity, acquisition session, cycle selection, potential windows, scan rates, currents, and frequencies.
  2. Extract model-independent descriptors Calculate CV loop measures, branch-separated currents, impedance descriptors, and resolved charge–discharge quantities.
  3. Fit rate relationships Compare candidate functions using consistent observation windows and normalization.
  4. Construct candidate storage networks Compare blocking, relaxing, distributed, and leakage-containing topologies.
  5. Simulate the experimental excitation Apply piecewise potential ramps or current steps with the stated initial conditions and switching rules.
  6. Fit shared parameters within each curve family Let rate dependence emerge from the model rather than fitting independent parameters at every rate.
  7. Assess identifiability and predictive checks Examine residuals, parameter stability, bootstrap intervals, and appropriate withheld observations.
  8. Verify the numerical implementation Compare limiting cases with analytical RC and leaky-capacitor expressions, and check CPE approximations against analytical impedance.
