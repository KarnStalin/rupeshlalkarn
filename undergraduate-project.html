Testing of Performance and Emission Characteristics of Plastic Pyrolysis Oil and Jatropha Blend in IC Engines

From fuel preparation to the engine test bench.

Study summary

Abstract

Adapted from the 2019 undergraduate project report for web presentation.

An experimental investigation evaluated Jatropha biodiesel–diesel blends and Plasto diesel blends (PDB) through fuel-property measurements, engine-performance testing, and smoke-opacity assessment. Thermophysical properties were examined for JB20, containing 20% Jatropha biodiesel by volume, and PDB10, containing 10% plastic-derived fuel by volume. Engine tests covered Jatropha blends up to 20% and Plasto diesel blends up to 15%, with diesel as the reference fuel.

The study compared indicated and brake power, thermal efficiency, specific fuel consumption, and fuel-consumption rate. The report found relatively small differences in brake power among the tested fuels and identified JB20 and PDB15 as promising blends within the evaluated performance measures. All tested blends were reported to pass the smoke-opacity test. Under the investigated conditions, the report described favourable performance of Plasto diesel blends relative to Jatropha blends.

These findings apply to the blend fractions, equipment, and operating conditions evaluated in the project. The emissions comparison was limited to smoke opacity; NOₓ, SOₓ, HC, and CO were not measured.

Experimental design

Two fuel families. A diesel reference.

Jatropha biodiesel + diesel

JB5 · JB10 · JB15 · JB20

5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% Jatropha biodiesel by volume; diesel makes up the remainder.

Plasto diesel blend (PDB)

PDB5 · PDB10 · PDB15

PDB means Plasto diesel blend. PDB5, PDB10, and PDB15 contain 5%, 10%, and 15% plastic-derived fuel by volume, respectively; diesel makes up the remainder.

The reported engine comparisons treat these as separate blend series, each tested against diesel.

Experimental workflow

Preparation, measurement, comparison.

  1. Prepare the fuels

    Plastic processing involved batch-reactor pyrolysis, filtration, and fractional distillation. The Jatropha pathway used transesterified oil for biodiesel blends.

  2. Characterize the samples

    Fuel-property evaluation included density, viscosity, flash and pour points, calorific value, and cetane assessment. FTIR supported comparison of chemical signatures.

  3. Test against diesel

    Compare blend behaviour under engine loading, recording performance and fuel-consumption measures. Smoke testing provided a separate exhaust-opacity assessment.

  4. Interpret the response

    Examine how power, efficiency, fuel use, and opacity vary with blend fraction and test conditions, alongside the measured fuel properties.

Test bench & analysis

What Was Evaluated

The performance-test setup was a single-cylinder, four-stroke, constant-speed, water-cooled diesel engine, rated at 3.50 kW at 1500 rpm, with a compression ratio of 18:1.

Power & torque
Indicated power, brake power, and torque as functions of engine load.
Efficiency
Indicated and brake thermal efficiency, mechanical efficiency, and volumetric efficiency.
Fuel consumption
Fuel-consumption rate and specific fuel consumption for the blend series and diesel reference.
Smoke opacity
Exhaust-smoke assessment using an RTM 430 opacimeter.

Emissions measurement scope

The project assessed smoke opacity. NOₓ, SOₓ, HC, and CO were not measured because a five-gas analyser was unavailable during the study.
