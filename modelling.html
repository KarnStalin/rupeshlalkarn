Descriptor Development

I develop mathematical descriptions of material structure, thermal conversion, light-induced heating, and electrochemical response. My work combines characterization-derived descriptors with physical equations, numerical simulation, and MATLAB-based analysis.

The modelling framework follows Ti₃AlCN MAX, acid-etched Ti₃CNTₓ, and DMSO-treated Ti₃CNTₓ through measurements of structure, surface chemistry, porosity, dispersion, and time-dependent behavior.

01 / Characterization to variables

Descriptor Extraction

Modelling experimental signals to obtain structural, chemical, interfacial, and dispersion descriptors.

Descriptor extraction begins with the measurement. I use baseline correction, spectral fitting, band integration, and physically defined transformations to convert characterization data into traceable quantitative variables.

XRD: diffraction-profile modelling

Baseline-corrected diffraction profiles are fitted with suitable peak functions, including pseudo-Voigt profiles. Peak centers, widths, and areas describe selected reflections. Residual inspection and information criteria support comparisons between alternative peak descriptions.

Icorrected(2θ) = Iraw(2θ) − Ibaseline(2θ) d = λ / (2 sin θ) Lc = Kλ / (β cos θ)

Descriptors: basal spacing, peak position, full width at half maximum, integrated peak area, apparent coherent stacking length, and normalized parent-region intensity.

The coherence estimate uses β in radians. Without instrumental-broadening correction, Lc remains an apparent stacking descriptor. The parent-region index is a comparative diffraction measure rather than a quantitative residual-phase fraction.

Raman: vibrational-feature modelling

Peak-profile fitting extracts center frequencies, linewidths, and integrated areas. Comparisons use corresponding spectral features, with attention to overlapping bands and mode identity across materials.

Δω = ωsample − ωreference Apeak = ∫ I(ω) dω Relative broadening = Γsample / Γreference

Descriptors: selected Raman shifts, fitted linewidths, relative broadening, and spectral area ratios. The selected normalized Raman shift supplies the vibrational variable in the composite descriptor framework.

FTIR: integrated spectral descriptors

Polynomial baseline correction and Savitzky–Golay smoothing support integration over defined spectral windows. Band areas provide comparative measures of broad, overlapping absorption features.

Bi = ∫[ν1 to ν2] Acorrected(ν) dν Rsurface = B2 / B1 Rdefect = B3 / B1 ROH = B4 / B1
Band Window Operational interpretation
B1 500–850 cm⁻¹ Lattice-related region
B2 850–1200 cm⁻¹ Termination-related region
B3 1350–1700 cm⁻¹ Defect- and adsorbate-sensitive region
B4 3000–3600 cm⁻¹ Hydroxyl- and interface-sensitive region

Descriptors: integrated areas, band-area fractions, and lattice-normalized ratios. These operational windows support comparison without treating each area as a unique chemical-species count.

XPS: chemical-envelope fitting

Survey quantification and high-resolution envelope fitting provide complementary chemical information. Background alternatives, constrained components, and linked spin–orbit partners guide the modelling of overlapping contributions.

Descriptors: quantifiable elemental ratios such as F/Ti, normalized chemical-envelope component fractions, and TiOₓ-like contributions. Candidate sulfur assignments are assessed using background-only descriptions and control samples.

Survey atomic percentages remain distinct from component fractions within a fitted envelope. Values below a reporting threshold are retained as censored information rather than automatically replaced with zero.

Gas sorption: accessible area and porosity

Surface area and pore-volume measurements supply geometric descriptors for the interface and effective-medium modelling framework.

Dp ≈ 4Vp / SBET φ = Vp ρbulk av = SBET ρbulk

Descriptors: specific surface area, pore volume, operational pore diameter, assumed-density pore fraction, and volumetric interface area. Density-dependent quantities are identified separately from measured inputs.

Zeta potential and DLS: dispersion variables

Zeta-potential magnitude describes an electrokinetic aspect of dispersion. DLS supplies an operational hydrodynamic size that can include aggregate behavior.

Electrokinetic variable = |ζ| Size variable = log10(Dh) Provisional combined index = |ζ| / Dh

Descriptors: zeta-potential magnitude, hydrodynamic diameter, transformed size, and combined dispersion measures. Hydrodynamic diameter is not interpreted as a direct lateral sheet dimension.

02 / Conversion and transport

Thermal Modelling

Event-specific thermal kinetics and effective-medium descriptions of heat transport.

Non-isothermal conversion kinetics

TGA and DTG signals define event boundaries, conversion intervals, and characteristic temperatures. Each selected event is normalized independently before kinetic analysis.

α(T) = [m0 − m(T)] / (m0 − mf) dα/dT = (A / β) exp[−Ea / (RT)] f(α) β = dT/dt

Here α is event-specific conversion, β is heating rate, Ea is apparent activation energy, and A is an apparent pre-exponential factor.

Kinetic treatments

Kissinger, Flynn–Wall–Ozawa, Kissinger–Akahira–Sunose, Starink, and Friedman formulations examine peak-temperature and conversion-dependent behavior.

Event reconstruction

Effective reaction-order descriptions reconstruct selected conversion intervals. Event overlap, derivative sensitivity, and heating-rate coverage guide interpretation.

Porosity and interface-controlled transport

Effective-medium calculations connect pore fraction, solid and pore-phase conductivity, and interfacial resistance. A Maxwell–Eucken treatment supplies the porous-medium conductivity term.

kporous = ks [2ks + kf − 2φ(ks − kf)] / [2ks + kf + φ(ks − kf)] keff = kporous / (1 + Rint av kporous) aeff = keff / (ρeff cp,eff) eeff = √(keff ρeff cp,eff)

ks and kf represent solid- and pore-phase conductivity. Rint is interface resistance, av is volumetric interface area, aeff is thermal diffusivity, and eeff is thermal effusivity.

Conductivity, diffusivity, and effusivity generated by this framework depend on specified physical assumptions. They remain identifiable as model outputs rather than direct conductivity measurements.

MATLAB implementation

The computational workflow combines event segmentation, conversion interpolation, derivative processing, kinetic regression, and reconstruction of thermal-conversion curves. Transport calculations retain consistent units and explicit material assumptions.

03 / Light-to-heat response

Photothermal Modelling

Energy balances, transient curve fitting, reference calibration, and heat-input estimation.

A transient energy-balance framework

Laser-induced heating is represented through a lumped thermal model. Effective heat capacity, heat input, and environmental heat loss determine the temperature evolution of the measurement system.

Cth dΔT/dt = Qin − hA ΔT

Cth represents effective thermal capacity and hA represents an effective heat-loss conductance. The temperature rise ΔT is defined relative to the ambient baseline.

Heating and cooling curve fitting

ΔTheating(t) = ΔT∞ [1 − exp(−t / τH)] ΔTcooling(t) = ΔToff exp[−(t − toff) / τC]

Separate heating and cooling fits provide temperature-rise amplitudes and characteristic time constants. The laser-switch-off temperature is kept distinct from an extrapolated asymptotic temperature.

Water-reference calibration

hAwater = Cth,water / τC,water Qnet,off = hAwater × (ΔTsample,off − ΔTwater,off)

A water reference provides a consistent calibration for reference-corrected net heat-input estimates under the measurement conditions.

Model quantity Role
Temperature-rise amplitude Describes the fitted transient magnitude
τH and τC Describe heating and cooling timescales
Transient area Integrates temperature rise over a defined interval
Net heat input Connects reference correction with the heat balance
Mass-specific heat input Normalizes the estimated heat input by material mass

Fitting and uncertainty

MATLAB-based nonlinear fitting is accompanied by residual inspection and assessment of temporal autocorrelation. Heat-balance estimates are distinguished from intrinsic photothermal conversion efficiency, which requires an absorbed-power measurement.

04 / Charge storage and relaxation

Electrochemical Modelling

CV rate analysis, EIS equivalent circuits, GCD reconstruction, and state-space simulation.

Cyclic voltammetry: branch and rate analysis

Anodic and cathodic branches support separation of background and symmetric current contributions. Scan-rate scaling examines operational current dependence at corresponding potentials.

ibg(V) = [ia(V) + ic(V)] / 2 is(V) = [ia(V) − ic(V)] / 2 is(V, ν) = a(V) ν^b(V) is(V, ν) = k1(V) ν + k2(V) ν^(1/2)

Loop-integrated capacitance, scan-rate exponents, and two-term decomposition coefficients are assessed within consistent potential windows and acquisition conditions.

EIS: equivalent-circuit descriptions

Candidate circuits represent series resistance, capacitive branches, distributed relaxation, and conductive pathways. Constant-phase elements describe non-ideal frequency dependence.

ZCPE(ω) = 1 / [Q (jω)^n] τRC = RC τR–CPE = (RQ)^(1/n) C*(ω) = 1 / [jω Z(ω)]

Resistance parameters, CPE coefficients, exponents, effective capacitance, and relaxation times provide complementary model quantities. The CPE coefficient Q is not directly equated with capacitance when n differs from one.

GCD: time-domain charge-storage modelling

Ccharge = I Δtcharge / ΔV Cdischarge = I Δtdischarge / ΔV q = ∫ I dt E = ∫ V I dt

Resolved charge and discharge segments support capacitance, charge-balance, and energy calculations. Rate-law comparisons examine current dependence with explicit potential-window and resistive-drop treatment.

State-space simulation

dx/dt = Ax + bI V = cᵀx + DI

Finite RC networks are represented in state-space form to simulate piecewise voltage ramps and constant-current segments. A CPE remains distinct from an exact finite RC realization.

Linear Kramers–Kronig checks, residual analysis, parameter identifiability, bootstrap uncertainty, and withheld scan rates, currents, or frequencies support model assessment across techniques.

05 / Connecting descriptors to response

Physics-Guided Descriptor Modelling

Screening, normalization, composite descriptors, physically motivated response functions, and model assessment.

This framework connects characterization-derived variables with response models while retaining their physical interpretation. Descriptor construction is separated from parameter fitting so that the origin and role of each variable remain traceable.

Normalization and directional alignment

N(x) = (x − xmin) / (xmax − xmin) Ninverse(x) = 1 − N(x)

Normalization places selected inputs on a common scale. Directional inversion is specified where required by the descriptor definition. Constant-valued inputs require separate treatment because their normalization denominator is zero.

Composite descriptor families

Family Inputs Purpose
Interfacial / structural score I Apparent Lc, FTIR B2 band-area fraction, F/Ti, and pore diameter Combines selected structural and interface-sensitive variables
Vibrational score D Selected Raman shift Represents a defined vibrational-change variable
Dispersion score S Log-transformed hydrodynamic diameter and zeta-potential magnitude Combines operational size and electrokinetic information
I = [1 − N(Lc) + N(B2 band-area fraction) + N(F/Ti) + N(Dp)] / 4 D = N(selected Raman-shift variable) S = [1 − N(log10(Dh)) + N(|ζ|)] / 2

These scores are study-specific comparative coordinates. Their definitions preserve the distinction between measured inputs, fitted characterization parameters, derived transformations, and composite variables.

Physically motivated response functions

Concentration enters explicitly rather than being absorbed into a material score. Linear, logarithmic, power-law, and saturation functions provide candidate descriptions of concentration-dependent response.

Example descriptor-based structure: Qnet = β0 + βC ln(C / C0) + βS S Alternative concentration structure: Qnet = Qmax C / (KC + C)

C0 is a reference concentration used to make the logarithm dimensionless. These symbolic forms describe model structure without presenting fitted coefficients.

Screening and sensitivity analysis

Screening examines missing or censored measurements, non-equivalent spectral features, redundant variables, acquisition-sensitive quantities, and dependence on assumed physical parameters.

Alternative FTIR descriptors, pore measures, and linear-versus-logarithmic size transformations test sensitivity to descriptor construction. Assumption-dependent thermal outputs are kept separate from predictor inputs when their use would create circular relationships.

MATLAB modelling workflow

  1. Build a traceable input table. Retain material identity, units, measurement origin, and processing history.
  2. Define the descriptors. Document normalization, direction, weighting, and treatment of incomplete measurements.
  3. Specify candidate models. Match mathematical forms to the physical question and available observations.
  4. Estimate parameters. Fit the chosen expressions and examine residual behavior and parameter stability.
  5. Assess model dependence. Compare information criteria, descriptor alternatives, uncertainty, and appropriate withheld observations.
  6. Verify the calculations. Check units, limiting behavior, reconstructed signals, and analytical–numerical consistency.
