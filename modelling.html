My thesis develops physics-guided descriptors and MATLAB workflows to connect MXene processing with thermal, photothermal, and electrochemical behaviour.

Three material states form the core comparison: Ti₃AlCN MAX phase, acid-etched Ti₃CNTₓ, and DMSO-treated Ti₃CNTₓ.

Descriptor development

Make the inputs physically interpretable.

Of 51 inventoried descriptors, 16 were retained for screening after physical and data-quality checks. Seven core quantities formed three material scores. Scores used fixed equal weights and did not use the primary photothermal response in their calculation.

How the final dispersion score is constructed

Each input is min–max normalized across the three materials. Hydrodynamic diameter Dh is log₁₀-transformed before normalization, and its normalized direction is inverted so smaller aggregates receive a higher score.

S = ½ [(1 − u) + v]

u = normalized log₁₀(Dh);
v = normalized |ζ|.

These are relative coordinates within the measured material set. They are not universal colloidal-stability scales.

Distinguishing descriptor definitions

The final predictor uses the logarithmic-size score from Section IV.12. It is distinct from the earlier provisional |ζ|/Dh ratio and the linear-size dispersion score. PDI-dependent descriptors could not be evaluated because PDI values were unavailable.

Thermal modelling

Separate structural estimates from kinetic evidence.

BET-informed thermal transport

Surface area, pore volume, and assumed density support derived porosity and interfacial-area descriptors. A Maxwell–Eucken treatment and an interfacial-resistance correction are used to examine effective thermal transport.

keff = kporous / (1 + Rint av kporous)

Rint: interfacial thermal resistance; av: interfacial area per unit volume. These conductivity and diffusivity values are model estimates dependent on assumed parameters, not direct thermal-transport measurements. They were excluded as predictors of the thermal response to avoid circularity.

Event-specific TGA kinetics

Multirate thermal analysis uses Kissinger and isoconversional methods, including FWO, KAS, and Starink, to examine apparent activation energies. Accepted events are reconstructed with an effective kinetic model.

DMSO event 2 gave reconstruction R² values of approximately 0.967–0.979 across the three heating rates. Several earlier events were poorly represented by a single first-order model; DMSO event 3 received no robust activation-energy recommendation.

Activation energies are event-level descriptors, not unique mechanistic constants. A successful reconstruction does not prove a first-order mechanism.

Electrochemical modelling

Three techniques. A shared test of the representation.

The thesis examines charge storage in acid-etched and DMSO-treated MXene electrodes before and after cycling using CV, EIS, and GCD. Model-independent descriptors, empirical rate laws, and equivalent-circuit representations are compared.

  1. Check data validity. Cycle stability, Kramers–Kronig consistency, acquisition sessions, and time resolution guide the usable measurement range.
  2. Compare representations. Models are assessed using effective-sample-size information criteria, identifiability checks, and withheld-data prediction.
  3. Quantify uncertainty. Bootstrap intervals and robustness tests assess which parameter changes are supported.
  4. Compare across techniques. CV and GCD select two relaxation time scales with a parallel conductive path; EIS resolves an additional time scale.

Slow-process capacitance agreed within 15% across techniques. The DMSO-treated electrode showed about 10% greater slow-timescale charge storage, while the most robust cycling change was a 54–72% increase in the acid-etched electrode’s slow-branch resistance.

Interpretation within the measurement regime

Each material was represented by one electrode, and capacitances were not mass-normalized. Circuit elements are effective representations rather than unique microscopic assignments. Small-signal EIS and large-signal CV/GCD probe different regimes.

MATLAB workflow and validation

Prediction has to survive being tested.

Photothermal workflow

  1. Prepare the nine measured response conditions.
  2. Screen descriptors and fix score definitions.
  3. Fit the pre-stated candidate models.
  4. Compare AICc and withheld-concentration / withheld-material errors.
  5. Evaluate coefficient intervals, residuals, sensitivity, and prediction intervals.

Uncertainty and scope

The photothermal analysis includes 20,000 Monte Carlo draws and an autocorrelation-aware uncertainty assessment. Descriptor scores remain fixed in the propagation because independent uncertainties are not available for every input.

The final model is a physics-guided calibration across three measured material states. Its applicability to other MXenes remains unestablished.

Although scores were computed without the primary response, the thesis notes that the qualitative material ranking was already known when the dispersion data were added.
