Understanding biodiesel preparation

Biodiesel consists of fatty acid alkyl esters, commonly produced by reacting oils or fats with an alcohol through transesterification. The following stages outline the general preparation process; the treatment required depends on the feedstock.

01 / PREPARATION

Feedstock preparation

Oils and fats are prepared for conversion. Filtration removes suspended material, while moisture and acidity help determine whether further pretreatment is needed.

02 / CONVERSION

Reaction and mixing

During transesterification, triglycerides react with an alcohol to form biodiesel and glycerol. Mixing and temperature control are important aspects of the process.

03 / RECOVERY

Separation and purification

After reaction, product phases are separated. Further purification and drying help prepare the biodiesel for fuel-property evaluation.

From preparation to fuel evaluation

Preparing biodiesel is the first step towards understanding its suitability as an alternative fuel. Properties such as viscosity, density, acidity, and moisture content help characterise the product. Engine testing provides a separate assessment of performance, combustion, and exhaust emissions.
